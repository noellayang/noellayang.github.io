Lighting Up Canada Data Pipeline

SOFTWARE & SYSTEMS INTERN, PBCM · JUNE 2026 – AUG 2026, REMOTE

view the map

About

A summer internship at Plant-Based Cities Movement, where I owned the full data pipeline behind their Lighting Up Canada map, from the research workflow to the review backend.

Tools

Google Apps Script, JavaScript, Leaflet, Git, technical documentation

What the project needed

PBCM wanted to map plant based and sustainability initiatives happening across Canadian cities, but the actual data collection was scattered across spreadsheets, half finished research, and a lot of manual copy pasting. My job was to turn that into something that could actually scale: a real pipeline, from finding new initiatives to getting them verified and onto a public map, without needing someone to babysit every step.

Building the pipeline

I designed and shipped the system end to end. On the backend, I built a review and approval workflow in Google Apps Script, since that was what the rest of the organization already worked in and could maintain without me. On top of that I built a reviewer facing web app where staff could search and filter incoming entries, check sources, review submitted images, and export approved entries to JSON with one click instead of manually formatting everything by hand.

The public side is a JavaScript and Leaflet map that reads from that exported data and plots every verified initiative across the country. I owned the architecture and the pipeline that feeds it, though the final HTML and CSS on the public map itself was built out by a teammate.

Scaling the research without losing the humans

The harder problem was finding new initiatives fast enough to keep up. I put together an AI assisted research workflow to help surface candidates each quarter, but I was careful about where the automation stopped. Every entry still needed human approval before it went anywhere near the public map, and I built in duplicate detection so the same initiative could not quietly get added twice from two different research passes.

Since I was not going to be the one running this every quarter, I wrote the technical SOP for the whole workflow and walked non-technical staff through it until they could run it independently. Handing off a system you built, and having it actually work without you standing over someone's shoulder, was its own kind of test.

What I'd flag

The public map itself is intentionally simple: pins, a search bar, a filter panel. It is not the flashiest thing to look at, but it is the visible tip of a pipeline that is doing more underneath, and it is still actively growing as more initiatives get reviewed and approved each quarter.
