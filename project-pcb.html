Adaptive Power Management Testbed & PCB

PERSONAL PROJECT · MAY 2026 – AUG 2026

github repo

About

ESP32-C3 power-management testbed built from my ChipSat power-budget work for Duke Solar Sail.

Tools

KiCad, ESP32-C3, soldering & rework, oscilloscope, INA219 current sensing, Fusion 360

Why I built it

While working on the ChipSat sensor subsystem, I was modeling eclipse survival, duty cycles, and battery recharge. I wanted a hardware check on those assumptions, so I built a board where I could keep the circuit fixed, change firmware states, and measure current directly.

What's on the board

The 45mm by 45mm, two-layer board uses an ESP32-C3-MINI-1 with native USB, BQ25185 solar/Li-ion charging, dual INA219 current monitors, a VEML7700 light sensor, and an ICM-42688-P IMU. A switchable +3V3_SENS rail lets firmware shut down the sensor domain, and a 36 ohm jumper-selected load provides an approximately 300mW transient for ADCS-scale testing.

I used the ESP32-C3 BLE radio as a telemetry proxy, with the same 780ms window and cadence used in the mission model. The goal was not to copy LoRa RF behavior, only to schedule around a real wireless event.

Firmware states

I tested three states: eclipse sleep, housekeeping with the sensor rail off, and full sensing. Keeping the hardware unchanged made it easy to isolate the effect of firmware scheduling.

Deep sleep reduced average current from 48.59mA to 2.25mA, a 95.4% drop. Full sensing measured 50.14mA. Enabling the 300mW switched load raised current from 26.02mA to 118.48mA, close to the resistive-load estimate.

BLE telemetry

To capture the cost of a radio event, I held the board in full sensing for 60 seconds and advertised for 780ms at 30-second intervals.

Across three trials, baseline current stayed at 53.67mA and brief BLE windows peaked at 121.5mA. At this cadence, radio activity occupies about 2.6% of the cycle.

Solar input

I paired the solar-side INA219 with the VEML7700 to compare harvested electrical power with measured illuminance.

Harvested power increased with illumination. The lowest-light point sits near the INA219 and 0.1 ohm shunt measurement floor, so I kept it in the plot but treated it as low-confidence data.

Bring-up notes

I brought the board up subsystem by subsystem: power rails, native USB, current monitors, light sensing, IMU, switched sensor rail, then the solar path. A few issues were worth documenting:

  • The ESP32-C3 uses one I2C controller across two physical buses. Cutting the sensor rail during a transaction could leave the bus stuck, so firmware now completes the transaction and releases SDA/SCL before power-down.
  • At low light, solar current approached the INA219 measurement floor and occasionally drifted around zero. I left those points in the dataset and marked the limitation.
  • Initializing BLE raised active current even with advertising disabled, so the firmware distinguishes "advertising off" from "BLE fully deinitialized."

Result

The final testbed can compare low-duty-cycle operation, full sensing, telemetry bursts, and short high-current events on the same hardware. The schematic, manufacturing files, BOM, firmware, and test notes are in the GitHub repo.
