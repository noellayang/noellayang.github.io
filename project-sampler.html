Autonomous Animal Saliva Sampler

EGR 101, FIRST YEAR DESIGN · DUKE UNIVERSITY

watch the demo video

About

First-year design project for autonomous wildlife saliva collection, built with a four-person team.

Team

Vlatko Stojkoski, Noella Yang, Salloni Gill, Hunt Cone

Problem

Our client wanted a way to collect wildlife saliva samples in remote areas without a person present at each interaction. We designed an automated device that could detect contact, expose a fresh sample surface, store the sample, and reset itself.

Design

A force-sensitive resistor detects contact at the opening and wakes the microcontroller. A linear actuator retracts the lid to expose filter paper mounted on film, then a stepper motor advances the film to a fresh section before the lid closes again. The mechanism stores at least ten samples before servicing.

I focused mainly on the sensing and control electronics: reading and debouncing the FSR trigger, then sequencing the linear actuator and stepper motor through each collection cycle.

Testing

We tested ten full collection cycles, repeated shake and water-exposure tests, and a 650 lb strength load. The device remained functional through each test. In a 20-person usability survey, 15 participants found the assembly instructions easy to follow.

Demo and presentation

We presented the final device at the Pratt Fair with a poster covering the design, mechanism, and test results. The demo video below shows one complete collection cycle.

Next iteration

The next revision would move the electronics onto a compact PCB, reduce the number of printed parts, and add a simple attractant holder near the sampling opening.
